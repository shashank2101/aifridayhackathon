# Dashboard Components Guide

This guide explains every visual component across the 4 pages of the application: what the component tells you, where it gets its data from, and why it was designed that way.

---

## Page 1: 🏭 Live Defect Risk Dashboard (`app.py`)

This is the main entry point and the "live" view that a factory floor supervisor would keep open on a monitor.

### 1. KPI Cards (Top Row)
- **What it shows:** 4 numbers: Total Processed, High Risk, Medium Risk, Needs Review.
- **How it's fetched:** It reads the live count from `data/alerts.csv`, which is constantly updated by the LangGraph pipeline as new batches arrive.
- **Why it's shown:** Gives a 2-second overview of the current factory floor health. If "Needs Review" is high, the supervisor knows the AI guardrails are blocking malformed outputs that require human attention.

### 2. Batch Risk Table
- **What it shows:** A color-coded table (Red=High, Yellow=Medium, Green=Low) showing recent production batches, their risk scores, and deviated parameters.
- **How it's fetched:** Loaded directly from `data/alerts.csv` and sorted by timestamp.
- **Why it's shown:** Acts as a triage queue. Operators can instantly see which machine is producing risky batches without clicking into anything.

### 3. Batch Drill-Down & AI Recommendation
- **What it shows:** Deep dive into a single selected risky batch. Shows the exact sensor readings, the AI's *Probable Cause*, and the *Recommended Action*.
- **How it's fetched:** The sensor readings come from the MES feed (`data/live_queue.csv`). The Cause and Action were generated by the LLM (Ollama) in the reasoning node of `graph.py` and saved to `alerts.csv`.
- **Why it's shown:** This is the core AI value proposition. It translates raw numbers (e.g., "Temp Diff = 6.2K") into actionable human instructions (e.g., "Inspect cooling fan").

### 4. Human-in-the-Loop Feedback Buttons
- **What it shows:** 👍 Helpful and 👎 Not helpful (with a popover for a written correction).
- **How it's fetched:** User clicks trigger Streamlit to append a new row to `data/feedback.csv`.
- **Why it's shown:** Closes the loop. Judges want to see that the AI system can learn from its mistakes. The written correction proves operators have a voice in retraining the system.

### 5. Chat with AI Assistant
- **What it shows:** A chatbot interface scoped *only* to the currently selected batch.
- **How it's fetched:** Passes the batch's specific sensor readings and risk profile as a hidden system prompt to the LLM backend.
- **Why it's shown:** Operators might need clarification on a recommended action (e.g., "Where is the cooling fan located on Machine M01?"). This proves the system is interactive.

## Page 2: 🔄 Producer (`pages/1_Producer.py`)

This page is purely for demo purposes. In a real factory, this page wouldn't exist—it represents the actual machines generating data.

### 1. Pool Statistics
- **What it shows:** How many batches are left in the simulation pool, and how many of them contain actual, hidden failures.
- **How it's fetched:** Reads `data/log_pool.csv`, which contains 500 rows of data the ML model has *never seen*.
- **Why it's shown:** Proves to judges that we are testing the model on truly unseen, live-streaming data, not just recycling training data.

### 2. "Push next batch" Button
- **What it shows:** A button to simulate time passing.
- **How it's fetched:** Copies rows from `log_pool.csv` and appends them to `live_queue.csv`.
- **Why it's shown:** Allows you to control the pace of the demo. You can push 5 batches at a time and watch the main dashboard instantly react and score them.

## Page 3: 📊 Quality Analytics (`pages/2_Analytics.py`)

This page shows historical, macro-level trends. It answers the question: *"How is our factory performing overall this quarter?"*

### 1. Defect & Repair Cost KPIs
- **What it shows:** Total historical defects, total repair cost, and average repair cost.
- **How it's fetched:** Aggregated from `data/defects_data.csv`, which logs historical breakdowns and their financial impact.
- **Why it's shown:** Ties manufacturing anomalies directly to business cost.

### 2. Failure Mode Breakdown
- **What it shows:** A bar chart showing the distribution of specific physics failures (e.g., TWF = Tool Wear Failure, HDF = Heat Dissipation).
- **How it's fetched:** Value counts from the `failure_mode` column in `defects_data.csv`.
- **Why it's shown:** Helps management understand structural issues. If HDF is the most common failure, the factory needs better cooling infrastructure.

### 3. Machine-Level Analysis
- **What it shows:** A table ranking machines by how much money they have cost the factory in repairs.
- **How it's fetched:** Grouping `defects_data.csv` by `machine_id` and summing the `repair_cost`.
- **Why it's shown:** Allows factory managers to identify "lemon" machines that need replacement rather than continued repair.

## Page 4: 📈 Success Metrics & ROI (`pages/3_Metrics.py`)

This page is built specifically to address the hackathon's "Success Metrics" requirement. It evaluates the AI itself.

### 1. Model Accuracy Metrics
- **What it shows:** Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1, and a Confusion Matrix.
- **How it's fetched:** Reads `model/metrics.json`, which was generated during model training (`train_model.py`) by comparing predictions against the held-out 20% test set.
- **Why it's shown:** Proves the ML model is scientifically valid. The Confusion Matrix specifically proves we optimized for high Recall (catching as many failures as possible), which is the correct economic choice for maintenance.

### 2. Defect Reduction & Cost Impact (ROI Simulator)
- **What it shows:** Calculates how much money the AI agent is saving the factory in real-time.
- **How it's fetched:** Looks at the actual failures in the unseen `log_pool.csv`, applies the model's Recall rate, and multiplies by the historical Average Repair Cost.
- **Why it's shown:** This is the ultimate "Mic Drop" for business stakeholders. It turns abstract AI accuracy percentages into concrete dollar amounts saved.

### 3. User Engagement & Feedback Loop
- **What it shows:** Total ratings, helpful ratio, and a table of written corrections.
- **How it's fetched:** Reads the data that operators generated via the thumbs up/down buttons on the main dashboard (`data/feedback.csv`).
- **Why it's shown:** Proves user adoption and trust. A high helpful ratio means the AI is generating good advice; written corrections prove operators are actively helping retrain the system.
